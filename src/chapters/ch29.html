Meta, NVIDIA and Amazon all spend enormous sums on AI, but they earn from it in completely different ways. Meta uses AI to make its social apps and ads better; for years it gave away its Llama models as open weights, then in 2026 switched to mostly proprietary Muse models. NVIDIA sells the GPUs and CUDA software that nearly every lab trains on, and now also releases its own open models. Amazon runs the largest cloud, sells many companies' models through Bedrock (including Claude), builds its own Nova models and Trainium chips, and is Anthropic's main cloud partner and a major investor.

29.2Meta and the Llama open-weights era

Meta has a long research history. Its lab FAIR (Facebook AI Research) was founded in 2013, with Yann LeCun as a founding director. Two FAIR contributions you will meet constantly:

  • PyTorch, the free deep-learning framework that started at Meta and is now used to build most research models.
  • Segment Anything (SAM), models that cut out objects in images.

Then came Llama. LLaMA 1 appeared in February 2023. Unlike GPT or Claude, its weights could be downloaded. Later versions became the default starting point for thousands of companies and hobbyists who wanted to run a capable model on their own machines or fine-tune it for their own data. Llama 4 followed on 5 April 2025.

Real-world example

A bank's QA team cannot send customer data to an outside AI service. In 2024 they download a Llama model, run it on an internal server, and use it to turn anonymised production logs into draft bug reports. Nothing leaves the building. This kind of "private" use was a big reason Llama was popular, and it is the main appeal of open-weight models generally (Chapter 32).

"Open weights" is not the same as "open source"

Llama models were released under Meta's own licences, not a standard open-source licence such as Apache 2.0 or MIT. On the Hugging Face Hub you can see this directly: Llama models carry licence tags like llama3.1 or llama3.2, which point to Meta's custom terms. You may download and use the weights, but you must follow those terms. A model licence is something a tester should check before a model goes anywhere near a product, exactly as you would check a third-party library.

29.3The shift to Muse (2026)

In 2025 Meta formed Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL), with Alexandr Wang as Chief AI Officer. Its first model changed Meta's direction.

  1. Feb 2023LLaMA 1, open weights for researchers.
  2. Apr 2025Llama 4 (5 April), the last Llama generation so far.
  3. Apr 2026Muse Spark (8 April), the first MSL model. Not open-weight: available through the Meta AI apps and a private-preview API.
  4. Jul–Sep 2026Muse Spark 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3 (9 Jul, 5 Aug, 2 Sep), all proprietary.
  5. Aug 2026Muse Glimmer (10 Aug), a 30-billion-parameter open-weight model under Apache 2.0. Muse Code, a terminal coding agent, also arrives in August.

When it launched Muse Spark, Meta said it "hope[s] to open-source future versions". So the honest summary is: Meta's frontier models are now proprietary, while it still releases some smaller open-weight models such as Muse Glimmer. Meta has not announced a next-generation Llama.

Reported but not verified

Secondary sources describe further releases called Muse Wonder (October 2026) and Muse Spark Open (reported as a very large open-weight Mixture-of-Experts model). The research for this book could not confirm their details or licence terms from Meta's own pages. If you see them in the lab below, read their model cards rather than relying on this book.

Opinion · technology press

Coverage at the time, for example VentureBeat's "Goodbye, Llama", framed Muse Spark as Meta stepping back from open weights at the frontier. Meta's own framing is about building "personal superintelligence" inside its apps. If you built on Llama, the practical point is unchanged: weights you already downloaded keep working under the licence they came with; what changes is where the newest models come from.

Meta's AI shows up where its users already are: the Meta AI assistant in WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, Messenger and Threads, the meta.ai app, and Ray-Ban Meta and Oakley Meta smart glasses. It is funded mainly by advertising, with optional paid tiers for Meta AI. Meta also runs very large NVIDIA GPU clusters, plus its own MTIA accelerator chips.

29.4NVIDIA: the engine room

NVIDIA was founded on 5 April 1993 by Jensen Huang (still CEO), Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem, to make graphics chips for games. Two things turned a graphics company into the centre of the AI industry.

  1. GPUs do many small sums at once. Drawing a game frame means calculating millions of pixels in parallel. Training a neural network means multiplying huge grids of numbers, which is the same kind of parallel work (Chapter 2).
  2. CUDA made that power usable. Released in 2006–07, CUDA let programmers use GPUs for general computing, not just graphics. In 2012, AlexNet, the image-recognition network that kicked off the deep-learning boom, was trained on NVIDIA GPUs.
In everyday life

A CPU is like a few brilliant professors who can solve any problem, one at a time. A GPU is like a stadium full of students who can each do simple arithmetic, all at once. For the giant, repetitive sums inside AI, the stadium wins. CUDA is the language the stadium understands, and almost everyone has learned it.

Alongside chips, NVIDIA sells DGX systems (complete AI servers), networking, and software such as NIM microservices for running models. On 29 October 2025 it became the first company to reach a $5 trillion market value.

Why is it so hard to compete with NVIDIA?

Rivals exist. Google has TPUs, Amazon has Trainium, Meta has MTIA, Microsoft has Maia, and AMD sells GPUs (OpenAI signed for 6 GW of AMD chips in October 2025). The difficulty is not only the hardware. Years of AI software (frameworks, libraries, tuned kernels, engineers' habits) were written for CUDA first. Moving to another chip means re-testing and often rewriting that software.

A tester will recognise the pattern: it is like moving a large automated test suite from one framework to another. The new tool may be cheaper or faster, but the migration, re-validation and retraining cost is real. That switching cost is what analysts usually mean by NVIDIA's "CUDA moat".

29.5NVIDIA moves up the stack

NVIDIA no longer only sells chips. It increasingly releases models and buys into the software layer.

  • Nemotron is NVIDIA's open-weight model family. Nemotron 3 was announced on 15 December 2025 in Nano, Super and Ultra sizes, using a hybrid Mixture-of-Experts design. Nemotron 3 Ultra has about 550 billion total parameters. Nemotron 3.5 Super VL (vision and language) has 120 billion total and 12 billion active parameters. Exact Nemotron 3.5 release dates were not verified.
  • Groq: in December 2025 NVIDIA licensed Groq's inference technology and hired key staff, in a deal of about $20 billion.
  • Hugging Face: on 2 September 2026 NVIDIA signed a definitive agreement to buy Hugging Face, the main public hub for open models and datasets, for about $12.9 billion. It had not closed as of 9 November 2026 and is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to regulators.
  • OpenAI and Anthropic: a 10 GW letter of intent with OpenAI (September 2025, later restructured), and an investment of up to $10 billion in Anthropic (November 2025).
Why the Hugging Face deal matters to you

Hugging Face hosts many of the open models and datasets testers use for experiments and local tools. Until the deal closes it remains independent, and nobody outside the companies knows what, if anything, will change afterwards. If your team depends on the Hub, note it as a supplier in your risk register and keep local copies of the models you rely on.

29.7Amazon and Anthropic

Amazon is Anthropic's closest infrastructure partner. Audit the licences of open models before you use them30 min · Python 3 or a browser

  2. Run python3 licence_audit.py. With no arguments it checks four organisations on the Hub: meta-llama (Llama), meta-models (Muse open models), nvidia and amazon.for each organisation, a count of licences across its 20 most-downloaded models, with custom licences flagged, as in the sample output.
  3. Look at meta-llama. Every licence is a Llama-specific one.tags such as llama3.1, llama3.2, llama2: open weights under custom terms, not a standard open-source licence.
  4. Look at meta-models. Find Muse Glimmer.Muse Glimmer shows apache-2.0, matching Meta's announcement. Any model tagged other needs its model card read.
  5. Look at amazon. Notice that Nova does not appear.Amazon's open models on the Hub are mostly forecasting and research models; Nova is proprietary and only available through AWS.

licence_audit.py (Python 3, standard library only)
"""Count the licences on each organisation's most-downloaded Hugging Face models."""
import json
import sys
import urllib.parse
import urllib.request
from collections import Counter

API = "https://huggingface.co/api/models"
STANDARD = {"apache-2.0", "mit", "bsd-3-clause", "bsd-2-clause"}


def top_models(author, limit=20):
    query = urllib.parse.urlencode({"author": author, "sort": "downloads", "limit": limit})
    with urllib.request.urlopen(f"{API}?{query}", timeout=20) as resp:
        return json.load(resp)


def licence_of(model):
    for tag in model.get("tags", []):
        if tag.startswith("license:"):
            return tag.split(":", 1)[1]
    return "(not stated)"


def main():
    authors = sys.argv[1:] or ["meta-llama", "meta-models", "nvidia", "amazon"]
    for author in authors:
        models = top_models(author)
        counts = Counter(licence_of(m) for m in models)
        print(f"\n== {author}: top {len(models)} models by downloads ==")
        for licence, n in counts.most_common():
            flag = "" if licence in STANDARD else "   <- read this licence before use"
            print(f"  {n:>3}  {licence}{flag}")
        custom = [m["id"] for m in models if licence_of(m) not in STANDARD][:3]
        if custom:
            print("  examples to check:", ", ".join(custom))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Two things are worth noticing. First, other simply means the licence is not one of the Hub's standard tags; it is a prompt to read, not a verdict. Second, cc-by-nc-4.0 includes "NC", meaning non-commercial: such a model may not be usable in a paid product. The script deliberately flags anything outside a short list of well-known permissive licences, the same way a test might flag anything outside an allow-list. It is a triage tool, not legal advice.

Troubleshooting
  • CERTIFICATE_VERIFY_FAILED on a Mac: run Install Certificates.command from your /Applications/Python 3.x/ folder once, then try again.
  • Network blocked at work: open https://huggingface.co/meta-llama (and the other organisations) in a browser, sort by "Most downloads" and read the licence on each model page.
  • HTTP Error 429: you have sent too many requests in a short time. Wait a minute and run it again.
  • An organisation shows "top 0 models": check the spelling; organisation names on the Hub are case-sensitive.
Common misunderstandings
"Llama is open source."
Llama weights are downloadable, but under Meta's custom licences, not a standard open-source licence. "Open weights" and "open source" are different things (Chapter 32).
"Meta stopped releasing open models."
Its frontier Muse Spark models are proprietary, but it released Muse Glimmer under Apache 2.0 in August 2026.
"NVIDIA is just a chip company."
It also sells full systems and software (CUDA, NIM), releases the open Nemotron models, and has agreed to buy Hugging Face (pending).
"Claude on Bedrock is a different, Amazon version of Claude."
It is the same Claude model family reached through AWS. Prices, available features and default aliases can differ, so check them.
"Amazon owns Anthropic."
Amazon is a major investor with a minority stake and Anthropic's primary cloud. Anthropic also works with Google and Microsoft.

29.8Review

Key takeaways
  • Meta earns from ads, NVIDIA from hardware and systems, Amazon from cloud usage; their AI choices follow from that.
  • Meta's Llama (2023–2025) made strong open weights mainstream, under custom licences. Since Muse Spark (April 2026) Meta's frontier models are proprietary, with some open models such as Muse Glimmer.
  • NVIDIA's GPUs plus CUDA underpin most AI training; generations run Ampere, Hopper, Blackwell and Vera Rubin.
  • NVIDIA now also releases Nemotron open models and has agreed to buy Hugging Face (about $12.9B, pending, expected first half of 2027).
  • Amazon offers Bedrock (many vendors' models, including Claude), its own Nova models and Trainium chips.
  • Amazon is Anthropic's primary cloud and a major investor; Claude Code can run on Bedrock with CLAUDE_CODE_USE_BEDROCK=1.
  • Always read a model's licence before use; licence tags on the Hub are a quick first check.
