Beyond the giants there is a crowded, fast-moving field. xAI builds the Grok models on its own huge computer cluster and is now part of SpaceX. DeepSeek, a Chinese research lab, showed in 2025 that very strong open models could be trained far more cheaply than people assumed. Mistral is Europe's leading independent lab, built around open weights and models customers can run themselves. Alibaba's Qwen family is among the most downloaded open models in the world. Around them sit specialists such as Cohere, Perplexity, Hugging Face, Moonshot, Zhipu and Apple. For a working team, this variety means real choice, and a duty to check licence, hosting and company policy before choosing.

30.1Why the wider field matters

It is easy to think AI means three or four American companies. In practice, many of the models your colleagues download, and some that power tools you already use, come from elsewhere. The wider field matters for four practical reasons:

ReasonWhat it means for a team
Open weightsMost of the strongest downloadable models in 2026 come from DeepSeek, Qwen, Mistral, Moonshot and Zhipu. If you need to run a model on your own servers, this is where many candidates are.
Price pressureCheap, capable models push every vendor's prices down, including for the models you already pay for.
Where data goesA company's hosted app or API sends data to that company's servers, in its country. Running its open weights on your own hardware does not. Policies often treat the two very differently.
Hidden dependenciesTools you use may be built on these models. For example, Cursor's Composer 2 and 2.5 coding models were reportedly built on Moonshot's Kimi K2.5.
In everyday life

Choosing an AI model is like choosing a supplier for a part in a car you build. Big-name suppliers are not the only option; smaller and foreign suppliers can be excellent and cheaper. But you still check the contract, where the part is made, and whether your company is allowed to buy from them.

30.2xAI and Grok (now SpaceX Super Intelligence)

Elon Musk incorporated xAI on 9 March 2023 and announced it publicly on 12 July 2023. It was based in Palo Alto, California. Its corporate story moved unusually fast:

  2. Mar 2024Grok-1 weights released under Apache 2.0 (17 March).
  3. 2025Merges with X Corp. (March). Grok 3 (February), Grok 4 (July) and Grok 4.1 (November) follow.
  4. Feb 2026SpaceX acquires xAI in an all-stock deal (announced 2 February).

  6. Jul 2026Grok Build (16 July), an Apache-licensed coding agent for the terminal.
  7. Sep 2026Grok 4.7, the current frontier model on the API (grok-4.7).

xAI's strategy is to train on very large self-built compute, the Colossus supercomputer in Memphis, built on NVIDIA GPUs, and to distribute Grok through X, the Grok apps, and Tesla and SpaceX products. SpaceX also owns Cursor

SpaceX also bought Anysphere, the maker of the Cursor code editor, for $60 billion (announced 16 June 2026, closed 14 August 2026). So one group now owns both a frontier model lab and one of the most popular AI coding editors. Chapter 26 covers this wave of consolidation.

30.3DeepSeek

DeepSeek was founded on 17 July 2023 by Liang Wenfeng in Hangzhou, China. It is owned and funded by the hedge fund High-Flyer. It behaves like a research lab: it publishes detailed papers and releases its models as open weights, mostly under the permissive MIT licence.

  1. May 2024DeepSeek-V2 introduces Multi-head Latent Attention, a way to cut memory use.
  2. Dec 2024DeepSeek-V3, a large Mixture-of-Experts model.
  3. Jan 2025DeepSeek-R1 (20 January), an open reasoning model that shook markets.
  4. Aug–Dec 2025V3.1 and V3.2 (with DeepSeek Sparse Attention).
  5. Apr 2026V4 preview (24 April): V4-Pro with 1.6 trillion total parameters, V4-Flash with 284 billion.
  6. Sep 2026V4.1-Flash (10 September). The API now serves deepseek-flash and deepseek-v4-pro.

Why R1 was such a shock

Before R1, many people assumed top reasoning models needed the budgets of the largest US labs. DeepSeek released a strong reasoning model openly, with a paper explaining how it was trained, and offered it cheaply. Investors started asking whether the huge spending on chips was really necessary, and AI-related share prices fell sharply at the time.

What DeepSeek's research actually showed
  • Reasoning from reinforcement learning. The R1 paper (arXiv 2501.12948) showed that a model called R1-Zero learned to reason step by step through pure reinforcement learning: it was rewarded for correct final answers on checkable problems such as maths, without first being shown worked examples. Long, self-checking chains of thought emerged on their own. Chapter 19 explains reasoning models.
  • GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization), from the earlier DeepSeekMath paper (arXiv 2402.03300). Instead of training a separate "critic" model to judge each answer, it compares a group of answers to the same question against each other. That saves memory and compute.
  • Efficiency tricks. Multi-head Latent Attention and fine-grained Mixture of Experts (DeepSeek-V2/V3, arXiv 2405.04434) cut the cost of running and training large models.

Other labs quickly adopted or adapted these ideas. That is how open research spreads: a published method becomes part of everyone's toolkit within months.

Chips. DeepSeek historically trained on NVIDIA GPUs (A100 and H800). V4 runs on Chinese chips from Huawei (Ascend) and Cambricon.

Check your policy, not the headlines

Using DeepSeek's own chat app or API sends your data to a company in China. Downloading its MIT-licensed weights and running them on your own servers does not. Many employers treat these two cases differently, and some forbid one or both. Before you paste a Jira ticket or a log file into any AI service, from any country, check your company's approved-tools list (Chapter 117).

30.5Alibaba's Qwen

Qwen is built by Alibaba Cloud, part of Alibaba Group (founded 1999 by Jack Ma and others, based in Hangzhou). It first launched as Tongyi Qianwen in April 2023. Its strategy has two halves: release a broad family of open-weight models to win developers, and keep the top "Max" models proprietary on Alibaba Cloud.

  • Releases in 2026: Qwen3.5 (February), Qwen3.6 (April), Qwen3.7 (May–June) and the Qwen3.8 family from August: Qwen3.8-Max (cloud only, 3 Aug), Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B (open weights, 12 Aug), Qwen3.8-27B (Apache 2.0, 14 Aug) and Qwen3.8-Flash (26 Aug).
  • No "Qwen 4" flagship had been confirmed as of 9 November 2026.
  • Reach: the Qwen app had 234 million users by May 2026, according to Wikipedia.

Qwen models are among the most downloaded open model families, and many community fine-tunes are built on them. Even NVIDIA publishes optimised versions of Qwen models on the Hugging Face Hub. A name such as Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B follows the Mixture-of-Experts pattern from Chapter 28: about 2.4 trillion total parameters, about 95 billion active.

Real-world example

A test-automation team wants a small model on each CI runner to classify failing tests as "product bug", "test bug" or "environment". They need something that fits in modest memory, allows commercial use and needs no outside API. An Apache 2.0 model such as Qwen3.8-27B is the kind of candidate they would shortlist, alongside options from Mistral, Google's Gemma or NVIDIA's Nemotron. They would then test each on a few hundred of their own past failures before choosing (Chapter 36).

30.8Turning a crowded market into a shortlist

With this many options, teams need a repeatable way to narrow down. A simple, defensible method has two stages, which testers will recognise as entry criteria followed by weighted scoring.

1. GatesPass/fail rules: commercial use allowed? Permitted by company policy (country, hosting, Entity List)?
2. EvidenceRun each surviving model on 50–200 of your own real tasks (Chapter 36)
3. Weighted scoreQuality, cost, hosting fit, licence comfort, vendor risk, each 1–5
4. Decision recordWrite down the winner, the runner-up and why

The point of gates is that no score can buy back a hard "no". A brilliant model your legal team will not approve is not a candidate. The lab turns this into a tiny script.

"""Score a model shortlist: hard gates first, then weighted scores (1 = poor, 5 = excellent)."""
import csv
import sys

WEIGHTS = {"quality": 0.35, "cost": 0.20, "hosting": 0.20, "licence": 0.15, "vendor": 0.10}
GATES = ["commercial_ok", "policy_ok"]  # every gate must be "yes" or the model is excluded


def load(path):
    with open(path, newline="", encoding="utf-8") as f:
        return list(csv.DictReader(f))


def score(row):
    return sum(float(row[k]) * w for k, w in WEIGHTS.items())


def main():
    path = sys.argv[1] if len(sys.argv) > 1 else "candidates.csv"
    rows = load(path)
    passed, failed = [], []
    for row in rows:
        missing = [g for g in GATES if row[g].strip().lower() != "yes"]
        (failed if missing else passed).append((row, missing))
    print(f"{'Model':<34}{'Score':>6}")
    for row, _ in sorted(passed, key=lambda p: score(p[0]), reverse=True):
        print(f"{row['model']:<34}{score(row):>6.2f}")
    for row, missing in failed:
        print(f"{row['model']:<34}{'out':>6}  (failed: {', '.join(missing)})")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Troubleshooting
  • FileNotFoundError: ... 'candidates.csv': run the command from the folder that holds the CSV, or pass its path: python3 shortlist.py path/to/candidates.csv.
  • KeyError: 'quality' (or another column): the CSV header row must match exactly, with no spaces after commas. Spreadsheet apps sometimes rename columns.
  • ValueError: could not convert string to float: a score cell is empty or contains text. Every score must be a number.
  • A model name contains a comma: wrap it in double quotes in the CSV, for example "Model, large".
  • Weights that don't add up to 1: the script still runs, but totals are no longer on a 1–5 scale. Keep them summing to 1 so scores are easy to read.
Common misunderstandings
"Open-weight models from China send data to China."
Weights you download and run on your own servers send nothing anywhere by themselves. Using a company's hosted app or API is different. Check your policy for each case separately.
"xAI is an independent start-up."
It merged with X in 2025 and was acquired by SpaceX in February 2026; it now operates as SpaceX Super Intelligence.
"Grok 5 is out."
As of 9 November 2026 the latest API model is Grok 4.7. Grok 5 had not been released.
"DeepSeek proved chips don't matter."
It showed that clever methods can greatly reduce cost. Its models still need large amounts of computing hardware; V4 runs on Huawei Ascend and Cambricon chips.
"All Mistral models are Apache 2.0."
Licences vary by model; Mistral Large 4 uses a custom open-weight licence.

30.9Review

Key takeaways
  • xAI (2023) builds Grok on its Colossus cluster; it merged with X, was bought by SpaceX in 2026 and now runs as SpaceXSI. Grok 4.7 is current; Grok 5 is not released.
  • DeepSeek (Hangzhou, 2023) releases efficient MIT-licensed open models; R1 (Jan 2025) showed reasoning could be taught by reinforcement learning, cheaply and openly.
  • Mistral (Paris, 2023) leads in Europe with open weights and self-hosting; Large 4 is about 1T parameters with 52B active, under a custom licence.
  • Alibaba's Qwen is a huge, mostly open family; the top Max models stay proprietary.
  • Cohere, Perplexity, Hugging Face, Moonshot, Zhipu and Apple each fill a niche, from enterprise search to on-device AI.
  • Choose with gates first (licence, policy), then evidence on your own tasks, then weighted scores, then a written decision.
