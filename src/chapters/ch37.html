Anthropic is the company that makes Claude. It was started in 2021 by a group of researchers, led by siblings Dario and Daniela Amodei, who believed powerful AI was coming and that safety research had to be done by people building the most capable models. To keep that aim in place as the company grew, they set it up as a Public Benefit Corporation and added an independent Long-Term Benefit Trust that elects a growing share of the Board. Building frontier models costs a fortune in computing power, so Anthropic depends on huge cloud partnerships with Amazon, Google and Microsoft, and on investors: its latest round, in May 2026, valued it at $965 billion. Knowing this background helps you understand why Claude behaves as it does and why the company publishes so much about safety.

37.1Why Anthropic exists

Every AI company tells a founding story. Anthropic's is unusual because its starting point was worry, not just opportunity. The founders believed three things at once:

  1. AI would become very powerful, quite soon. Several founders had worked on the research showing that language models get steadily better as you make them bigger and train them on more data (the "scaling" idea you met in Chapter 7).
  2. Powerful AI could cause serious harm if it was misused or behaved in ways nobody intended.
  3. You can only study the safety of frontier AI if you build frontier AI. Safety research on small, old models may not tell you how the newest ones behave.

Put those together and you get Anthropic's core bet: a safety-focused lab should be at the cutting edge, so it can do the research, set an example, and influence how the rest of the industry behaves. Critics point out the tension in this bet (a safety lab that also races to build more capable models), and Anthropic's leaders talk about that tension openly. Section 37.6 returns to it.

In everyday life

Imagine a group of structural engineers who think a new kind of very tall building is about to be built everywhere. They could write warnings from the sidelines. Instead they start their own construction firm, so they can test new safety methods on real towers, publish what they learn, and show that safe towers can also be good business. Whether that works depends on whether the firm stays true to safety when deadlines and money press on it. That is exactly the question people ask about Anthropic.

37.3The mission and the Public Benefit Corporation

Most companies are legally expected to act in the interests of their shareholders. A Public Benefit Corporation (PBC) is still a for-profit company, but its directors must also weigh a stated public purpose. Anthropic is a Delaware PBC. Its company page gives its purpose as:

Anthropic's stated purpose

"the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity"

In practice this gives the Board legal room to make choices that cost money in the short term, such as delaying a release for more safety testing, without being accused of ignoring shareholders. It does not force any particular decision, and it does not make the company a charity.

An ordinary company

Directors focus on shareholder value. A safety delay that lowers profits can be hard to defend to investors.

A Public Benefit Corporation

Directors must balance shareholder value with the stated public benefit. A safety delay can be justified by the company's purpose. Investors knew this when they invested.

37.4The Long-Term Benefit Trust and the Board

A PBC alone still leaves the Board chosen by shareholders. Anthropic added a second layer, announced on 19 September 2023: the Long-Term Benefit Trust (LTBT).

  • The Trust is an independent body of trustees who have no financial stake in Anthropic.
  • It can elect a growing share of the Board. When it was announced, Anthropic said the Trust was expected to elect a majority of the Board within four years.
  • It does not take part in day-to-day decisions. It shapes who sits on the Board, and the Board oversees the company.

Under the current Responsible Scaling Policy (Chapter 38), the Trust also has a formal safety role: it can request external review of Anthropic's risk reports, approve the external reviewers, and receives regular briefings.

In everyday life

Think of a football club owned by investors, with an independent supporters' trust that has the right to appoint more and more of the club's board. The trust doesn't pick the team on Saturday, but over time it decides who makes the big decisions, so the club can't easily be turned into something its supporters never wanted.

Under the hood: why the Trust's power grows gradually

Giving an outside body control of the Board on day one would have made the company hard to fund: investors would be handing money to a company they could not influence. Phasing in the Trust's power let Anthropic raise money while committing, in advance, to a structure where independent trustees end up with a strong say.

The 2023 announcement also described safeguards around changing the arrangement. The details are in the announcement post. This book does not summarise them line by line, because the exact mechanics are legal text best read at the source.

Is it enough? People disagree. Supporters say it is one of the strongest governance commitments of any frontier lab. Sceptics note that trustees depend on information from the company, and that very large investors still have influence. Both views are opinions, not settled facts.

Real-world example: why this matters to your team

Your company may already have an AWS, Google Cloud or Azure contract. Because of these partnerships, you can usually reach Claude through the cloud you already use (Bedrock, Google Cloud's Vertex AI, or Microsoft Foundry) instead of signing a new supplier contract. For a QA team that wants to use Claude in CI, that can turn a three-month procurement process into a configuration change. Chapter 41 compares these routes.

This multi-cloud approach is unusual. Anthropic runs on three different kinds of AI chip: NVIDIA GPUs, Google TPUs and Amazon Trainium. Spreading work across them gives it more capacity and bargaining power, at the cost of engineering effort to make models run well on each.

Under the hood: reading a funding headline

Post-money valuation means the company's value including the new money. If investors put in $65B and the post-money value is $965B, the company was valued at $900B just before the round.

"Up to" figures (Amazon's "up to $4 billion", NVIDIA's "up to $10 billion") are ceilings, often paid in stages or tied to conditions. They are not cash in the bank on the day of the announcement.

Compute commitments (Anthropic's $30B to Azure) run the other way: they are money Anthropic agrees to spend with a partner, usually over several years. Headlines sometimes blur investment in and spending out. When you read about an AI deal, ask: who pays whom, how much is conditional, and over what period?

Opinion · supporters and critics

Supporters, including Anthropic's leadership in its public writing, argue that having a safety-focused lab at the frontier raises standards across the industry: competitors adopt similar scaling policies, publish system cards, and take interpretability seriously. Critics, including some AI-safety researchers and commentators, argue that any lab racing to build more capable models speeds up the very risks it warns about, and that commercial pressure will eventually win over caution. This book does not take a side. As a reader, judge the company by what it publishes and does over time, not by its slogans or its critics' slogans.

Common misunderstandings
"Anthropic is a non-profit."
It is a for-profit Public Benefit Corporation. It sells products and raises investment; its directors must also weigh its stated public purpose.
"Amazon (or Google) owns Anthropic."
Amazon announced a minority stake; Google, Microsoft and NVIDIA are partners or investors too. None of them is described as owning the company.
"The Long-Term Benefit Trust runs the company."
It elects a growing share of the Board and has some safety-review powers, but it does not make day-to-day decisions.
"A $965B valuation means Anthropic has $965B."
Valuation is the price investors agreed the whole company is worth. The cash raised in Series H was $65B.
"Claude always knows the latest facts about Anthropic."
Claude's built-in knowledge stops at its training cutoff. For anything recent, use web search or check the official pages.
