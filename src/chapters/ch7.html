Before you start: Chapter 6 (the Transformer and GPT).

From 2018, researchers found that making Transformer models bigger, and training them on more text, kept making them better, often in surprising ways. In late 2022 OpenAI put a model that had been trained to follow instructions into a simple chat window, called it ChatGPT, and the world noticed. A race followed: Claude, Gemini, open models anyone could download, models that "think" before answering, and by 2025, agents that do multi-step work on their own. This chapter tells that story year by year.

A parameter is one of the adjustable numbers inside a model (Chapter 11). GPT-3 had about a thousand times more than GPT-1.

Scaling laws (2020)

In January 2020 OpenAI researchers led by Jared Kaplan published "Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models". They found that a model's error fell smoothly and predictably as you increased three things: the model's size, the amount of training data and the computing power used. In plain words: you could forecast how much better a model would get if you spent more. That turned AI progress into an engineering and investment question, and set off the race to build ever larger models. (Kaplan later co-founded Anthropic.)

In everyday life

Imagine discovering that every time you double the size of a library and the time a student spends reading in it, their exam score improves by a predictable amount. Suddenly building bigger libraries looks like a sure bet. That's what scaling laws did for AI investment.

Larger models also showed abilities that smaller ones lacked, such as basic arithmetic, translating between language pairs they'd rarely seen, or writing simple code. Researchers called these emergent abilities, though how sudden they really are is still debated.

7.2From predicting text to following instructions (2021–2022)

A model trained only to predict the next word isn't a helpful assistant. Ask it "How do I bake bread?" and it might continue with "How do I bake cake? How do I bake biscuits?", because lists of questions are common online. It needs a second stage of training to behave like an assistant.

In 2022 OpenAI published InstructGPT, which used reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). People wrote example answers and ranked the model's answers from best to worst, and the model was trained towards the preferred kind. The result: a much smaller model that people preferred over the giant GPT-3, because it actually did what was asked. Chapter 18 explains how this works.

Meanwhile, generative AI broke out beyond text:

  1. 2021DALL·E (OpenAI) generates images from text descriptions. GitHub Copilot preview suggests code inside programmers' editors, powered by OpenAI's Codex model.
  2. 2022DALL·E 2, Midjourney and Stable Diffusion make high-quality image generation widely available; Stable Diffusion's weights are released publicly.

7.3November 2022: ChatGPT

On 30 November 2022 OpenAI released ChatGPT as a free "research preview". The underlying model wasn't dramatically new. It was an instruction-tuned GPT-3.5 model. What changed was the packaging:

  • A chat window anyone could use, with no technical setup or API key.
  • Conversation: it remembered what you said earlier in the chat, so you could refine requests.
  • Free, at least to start.
  • Trained to be helpful through RLHF, so it followed instructions instead of rambling.

It spread faster than almost any consumer product before it. One widely reported analyst estimate put it at around 100 million monthly users within about two months. For many people, this was their first experience of AI that felt like talking to something.

Real-world lesson

ChatGPT's success is a lesson in product design, not just science. Similar models existed, but they were hidden behind developer tools. Making a capable model easy, conversational and safe enough for the public is what changed the world. That's why this book spends as much time on how to use AI products as on how they work.

7.62025: reasoning goes open, and agents arrive

  1. JanDeepSeek-R1, from a Chinese lab, matches leading reasoning models on many tests and is released with open weights. Its reportedly low training cost briefly shook technology stock markets.
  2. 24 FebClaude 3.7 Sonnet, described by Anthropic as the first "hybrid reasoning" model (it can answer quickly or think at length), and Claude Code as a research preview: an agent that works in your terminal.
  3. 22 MayClaude 4 (Opus 4 and Sonnet 4). Claude Code becomes generally available.
  4. AugGPT-5 (OpenAI). Claude Opus 4.1 (5 Aug).
  5. Sep–NovClaude Sonnet 4.5 (29 Sep), Haiku 4.5 (15 Oct) and Opus 4.5 (24 Nov); Gemini 3 (18 Nov). Coding agents from every major lab compete on real software tasks.

2025 is often described as the year of agents. Instead of answering one question, AI systems started working through tasks of many steps: reading code, running tests, fixing failures and reporting back. That's the shift from "era 2: create" to "era 3: act".

Common misunderstandings
"ChatGPT was the first large language model."
GPT-3, BERT and others came before. ChatGPT was the first that became a mass-market product.
"Bigger is always better."
Scaling helps, but smaller, well-trained models often beat older, bigger ones, and speed and cost matter for real work (Chapter 31).
"Claude is built on ChatGPT."
No. Claude is Anthropic's own family of models. Both use the Transformer design and similar training ideas, but they're separate systems from separate companies.
"The newest model is always the best for my task."
The right model depends on the task, speed and budget. Chapter 35 walks through real choices.

7.9Review

Key takeaways
  • GPT-2 and GPT-3 showed that larger models trained on more text keep improving; scaling laws (2020) made the gains predictable.
  • Instruction tuning with RLHF (InstructGPT, 2022) turned text predictors into helpful assistants.
  • ChatGPT (30 Nov 2022) succeeded through easy, conversational, free packaging of an instruction-tuned model.
  • 2023 brought Claude, GPT-4, Bard/Gemini and open-weight Llama; 2024 brought model families, Artifacts, reasoning models, computer use and MCP.
  • 2025 was the year of reasoning going open (DeepSeek-R1) and of agents, including Claude Code.
  • In 2026 Anthropic added a top tier, Fable; the current lineup (verified Nov 2026) is Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5.
