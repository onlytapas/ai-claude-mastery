An artifact is a piece of finished work that Claude puts in its own panel next to the conversation, instead of burying it in the chat: a document, a slide deck, a diagram, a dashboard or a small working web page. You can keep improving it by asking for changes, edit it directly, export it, and share it with colleagues. Some artifacts are little apps: they can call Claude, use your connected apps, and remember data between visits. For testers, that means you can describe a tool you need, such as a release checklist or a results dashboard, and have a working version in minutes. They are quick tools, not production software, and what you share can reveal more than you intend.

48.1What an artifact is

Anthropic introduced artifacts with Claude 3.5 Sonnet in June 2024, and they are available on every plan today. The help centre defines an artifact as content Claude makes that you could show to others. It opens beside your conversation, and you can edit it, come back to it and share it.

Claude decides when to make one. According to the help centre, it does so when the content is:

  • significant and self-contained, typically more than 15 lines;
  • likely to be edited, reused or used outside the conversation;
  • understandable without the rest of the conversation;
  • something you're likely to refer back to.

You can also just ask: "Make this an artifact" or "Build this as an interactive page". You need the "Code execution and file creation" capability on (shown as "Create and edit files" in the updated settings layout), because artifacts run in the same environment as file creation. On Team and Enterprise, owners control it under Organization settings > Capabilities.

In everyday life

A chat reply is like talking something through with a colleague. An artifact is the whiteboard next to you: the thing you're both working on stays in view, you keep adjusting it, and at the end you take a photo (export) or let others look at it (share). Some whiteboards are smart: you can tick boxes on them, and they remember.

New and legacy artifacts

Anthropic reworked artifacts in 2026. Artifacts made in a chat before 16 September 2026 are now called legacy artifacts: they keep working and can still be published and shared, but you can't create new ones of that kind. Artifacts made in Claude Cowork before 19 August 2026 are "live artifacts" and can't be edited in place. Some settings and sharing buttons differ between legacy and new artifacts, and older guides online describe the legacy behaviour. Artifacts are also available in Claude Code, on every plan that includes Claude Code.

48.3Editing, versions and export

You improve an artifact the same way you got it: by asking. "Add a column for owner", "make the failed rows red", "split this into two slides". In template artifacts (docs, slides, designs) you can also edit directly. In a doc or Markdown document you can highlight text and choose "Edit with Claude" to change just that part. If an interactive artifact shows an error, a "Try fixing with Claude" option asks Claude to repair it; the help centre notes this doesn't always succeed.

If you edit an earlier message in the chat, you create a separate branch of the conversation with its own artifacts. That's useful for trying two designs, and confusing if you forget which branch you're on.

Real-world example

Before a release go/no-go meeting, a QA lead pastes the test summary, open S1 and S2 bugs and known risks into a chat and asks for a five-slide deck. She edits two slides by hand, asks Claude to turn a table into a chart, and exports to PowerPoint for the company template. Twenty minutes instead of an hour, and the numbers come straight from her data rather than retyped.

48.4Artifacts that act: Claude, apps and storage

Beyond static content, an artifact can be a small app with three extra abilities.

Using Claude

An artifact can call Claude itself, so the people using it interact with Claude through your page. No API key is needed and it costs the creator nothing: usage counts against each user's own plan. New artifacts ask permission the first time they use Claude. For legacy artifacts there is a setting, "AI-powered artifacts" (now "Artifacts that use Claude"), under Settings > Capabilities.

Connecting apps

An artifact can use your connected apps (the help centre's examples are Asana, Google Calendar and Slack) and custom connectors. The first use asks for approval, and you can turn off individual tools. Connector tools that need approval for every action aren't available to artifacts. Each person connects their own apps, and Team and Enterprise owners can turn this off.

Storing data

Persistent storage lets an artifact remember data between visits: ticked checklist items, a log of exploratory sessions, survey answers. It is on Pro, Max, Ultra, Team and Enterprise, on web and desktop.

QuestionAnswer (help centre, November 2026)
Who sees the data?Personal storage is private to each user. Shared storage is visible to everyone using the artifact; Claude asks you to confirm the first time.
How much?20 MB per artifact, text only: no images, files or binary data.
Must I publish first?New artifacts: no. Legacy artifacts: yes, storage only works once published.
What deletes it?Unpublishing a legacy artifact permanently deletes its stored data, personal and shared.
Check which storage before typing anything sensitive

A checklist with shared storage shows your entries to everyone who opens it. The help centre advises checking which kind of storage an artifact uses before entering sensitive information. For QA tools, store test IDs and statuses, not customer data or credentials.

Under the hood: what an interactive artifact really is

An interactive artifact is a small web page, written by Claude in HTML, CSS and JavaScript (sometimes using a framework such as React), that runs in a protected area of your browser, separate from the rest of claude.ai. When you click a button, your browser runs that code; Claude isn't involved unless the page calls it.

In general terms, the extra abilities work through permissions the page is granted by the Claude app: a way to ask Claude a question, a way to call your approved connector tools, and a small store for saved data. (This is a simplified picture; Anthropic doesn't publish every detail.) That is why each ability asks for approval on first use, why connector tools needing per-action approval are excluded (a page can't stop and ask you in the middle), and why usage is counted against whoever is using the page.

Because it's generated code, an interactive artifact can contain bugs like any other software. Test it like software: try empty inputs, very long text and odd characters before trusting it.

Artifacts start private to you. What happens next depends on your plan and whether the artifact is new or legacy. Sharing settings need Claude on the web or desktop; on mobile you can view and edit, but not change sharing.

A published legacy artifact can be embedded in another website: choose "Get embed code" and list the "Allowed domains". After you unpublish, you can't republish that same artifact; you'd create a new one.

Sharing can reveal more than the artifact

The help centre warns that people who view a shared chat artifact can also get access to the attachments and files in that chat. If you uploaded a raw log or a customer spreadsheet to build a dashboard, check before sharing. Rebuild the artifact in a clean chat with masked data if in doubt. Also, if the artifact was made inside a project, viewers need access to that project.

Opinion · this textbook's authors

Treat "Anyone with the link" as "public". Links get forwarded and pasted into tickets. For anything about unreleased features, defects or customers, keep sharing inside your organisation.

Know the limits

An artifact is good for personal and small-team tools. It isn't a replacement for your test management system, your CI dashboard or anything audited. Data storage is capped at 20 MB of text per artifact, connector tools that need per-action approval can't be used, and the help centre doesn't promise uptime or versioned releases. If a tool becomes important to the team, give it a proper home.

Common misunderstandings
"Artifacts are just code blocks in a side panel."
They include documents, slides, designs, dashboards and working apps, with editing, export and sharing.
"If I share an artifact that uses Claude, I pay for everyone's use."
Usage counts against each user's own plan, and no API key is needed.
"Sharing an artifact shares only the artifact."
For shared chat artifacts, viewers can also get access to the chat's attachments. Check before sharing.
"Data in an artifact is private."
Personal storage is private to each user; shared storage is visible to everyone who uses the artifact.
"Unpublishing is like pausing."
For legacy artifacts, unpublishing permanently deletes stored data and you can't republish the same artifact.

48.7Review

Key takeaways
  • An artifact is substantial, self-contained work shown beside the chat that you can edit, export and share. It needs the code execution and file creation capability.
  • Kinds include documents, code, diagrams, web pages and tools, plus templates for Docs, Slides and Design on all plans; Dashboards and Motion are beta with plan limits.
  • Artifacts can use Claude (usage on each user's plan), connect apps and store data (20 MB of text, personal or shared) on paid plans.
  • Individual plans share with "Anyone with the link"; Team and Enterprise keep artifacts inside the organisation unless owners allow external sharing.
  • Shared chat artifacts can expose chat attachments, so build shareable tools with masked data.
  • Test AI-built tools like any software, and give important tools a proper home.

Assignment

Pick one repetitive QA chore in your team (for example, compiling the release summary, generating boundary values, or logging exploratory sessions). Build an artifact for it, test it with at least five edge cases and record what broke and how you fixed it. Decide and write down: who should see it, which storage it uses, and whether its data is safe to share. Demo it to your team and collect one improvement request.

Teach it

"An artifact is Claude's finished work in its own panel: a doc, a deck, a diagram, or a small working tool. You keep asking for changes until it's right, then export or share it. Some artifacts are little apps that can call Claude, use your connected apps and remember data. For QA, that means a checklist or a results dashboard in minutes. Two cautions: test it like any software, and remember that sharing can expose more than you think, including the files you uploaded in that chat."
