Individuals buy Claude on personal plans; organisations buy Team or Enterprise, or use the API and cloud platforms. The organisation plans add what a company needs: central billing, single sign-on, controls over which connectors and features people can use, usage reports and, on Enterprise, automated user management, audit logs and custom data retention. Commercial plans are not used to train models by default. For Claude Code, administrators can push managed settings that no individual can override. Prices and features change often and differ by plan, region and what your admin switched on, so always check the current pricing page and your own admin console.

Usage limits are not a fixed message count

Anthropic's pricing page explains: "How fast you reach a limit depends on the model you use, how long your chats are, and how large your files are." A day of Opus with large log files uses your allowance far faster than short Haiku questions. Chapter 43 shows how to see your usage.

In everyday life

Personal plans are like a personal phone contract: you choose it, pay for it and manage it. Team and Enterprise are like a company phone fleet. IT decides which apps are allowed, sets up logins through the company directory, sees usage across the fleet and can wipe access when someone leaves. The phone (the model) is identical; the management around it is what you pay extra for.

42.2Why an organisation needs more than personal plans

If fifty testers each buy their own Pro plan, they get the same models. What the company doesn't get:

  • Data under the company's agreement. Personal plans follow consumer terms, where each person chooses whether their chats help train models. Team and Enterprise follow commercial terms, where training is off by default (42.4).
  • Control of who has access. When someone leaves, a personal account leaves with them, chat history and all.
  • Policy. No way to switch off a risky connector, require single sign-on or limit sharing.
  • Visibility and one bill. No combined view of usage or spend, and fifty expense claims.
Real-world example

A QA department of 30 people (illustrative) started with personal Pro plans. A security review found test data containing real customer names in several personal chat histories. The department moved to a Team plan with SSO tied to the company directory, disabled every connector except Jira and Confluence, and turned off member feedback submissions. A one-page usage guide told people which data could go into Claude. The model was the same; the risk was much lower.

42.3The admin console: what administrators control

Team and Enterprise organisations are managed from an administration area in claude.ai, usually called the admin console. Owners (the Primary Owner and other Owners) and, on Enterprise, other roles you define use it to run the organisation. The exact menus change often, so this section describes the capabilities rather than button names.

Identity: SSO, SCIM and domains

  • Single sign-on (SSO) lets people log in with their company account (for example through Okta or Microsoft Entra ID) instead of a separate Claude password. Available on Team and Enterprise.
  • Domain verification proves your company owns its email domain. Team and Enterprise.
  • SCIM (System for Cross-domain Identity Management) is a standard that lets your company directory create, update and remove Claude accounts automatically. When HR marks someone as a leaver, their Claude access goes too. Enterprise only.
  • Domain capture brings personal accounts created with a company email address into the organisation. Enterprise only.

Features, connectors and sharing

  • Connector controls: decide which connectors members may use (Team and Enterprise). This is the main way to stop company data flowing to unapproved third-party services.
  • Web search: on Team and Enterprise an Owner must switch it on before members can use it.
  • Feedback: Owners can turn off members' thumbs up/down submissions. That matters because submitted feedback is the main way commercial conversations can end up in training data.
  • Sharing: on Team and Enterprise, chats can only be shared inside the organisation. Projects can be shared with colleagues.
  • Organisation-wide skills: publish approved skills (such as your bug-report template) to everyone.

Visibility and spending

  • Usage analytics show who is using Claude and how much (Team and Enterprise). Useful for licence decisions and for spotting where training would help (Chapter 126).
  • Seats: choose Standard or Premium seats on Team; move heavy users to Premium.
  • Spend limits (Enterprise) cap usage costs, which matters because Enterprise usage is billed at API rates on top of seats.
  • Audit logs (Enterprise) record administrative and user events for security investigations.
  • Compliance API (Enterprise) gives programmatic access to usage and content data for compliance tooling.
  • IP allowlisting (Enterprise) restricts access to approved company networks.

The API side: the Console and workspaces

If your organisation also uses the API, that is managed separately in the Claude Console at platform.claude.com. There you create API keys, invite members and set spending limits. Workspaces group keys, members and limits; an organisation can have up to 100. A sensible pattern is one workspace per team or system, for example "qa-ci" for the nightly failure-triage job, so you can see and cap its cost separately. If you use Bedrock, Google Cloud or Foundry instead, access and billing are managed in that cloud's own console.

Features vary

What you see depends on your plan, your region and what your administrators enabled. Many features launch in beta on some plans first (for example, Claude Tag is in beta for Team and Enterprise). If a colleague's screen shows a feature yours doesn't, the reason is usually an admin setting, not a bug. Ask your Owner before assuming it's broken.

Under the hood: what "zero data retention" does and doesn't mean

ZDR is a contractual arrangement in which Anthropic agrees not to store inputs and outputs beyond what is needed to process the request, instead of the standard 30-day deletion. The privacy centre describes it as an agreement on terms "other than" the standard deletion and points to a separate article for which products it covers. Three practical points: it is something you agree with Anthropic, not a switch you flip; it may not cover every product or feature (features that store data by design, such as the Files API, keep data under your control until you delete it); and Usage Policy enforcement and legal requirements can still apply. If ZDR matters to you, get the current scope in writing from your account team.

Cloud platforms

If you reach Claude through Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud or Microsoft Foundry, your agreement with that provider governs data handling. Your cloud security team will already know how to review that, which is one reason enterprises like this route.

Opinion · widely held among security practitioners

Many security teams argue that contract terms are only half the control, and that the bigger risk is people putting the wrong data in. Their usual advice: approve one route, block the others where you can, give staff a short "what may go in" rule, and provide masked test data so people don't need production data. This is practitioner judgement rather than a published standard; Chapter 132 and Chapter 135 cover privacy and governance in depth.

42.5Managed settings for Claude Code

Claude Code reads settings from several files: your personal settings, the project's shared settings and your local overrides (Chapter 78). Above all of them sit managed settings, which the organisation deploys. Claude Code's documentation is explicit: "Nothing in your own settings files or --settings overrides a managed key."

How managed settings reach a machine

  • Server-managed settings from the claude.ai admin console (or a self-hosted Claude apps gateway, started with claude gateway). These also reach Claude Code cloud sessions.
  • MDM or operating-system policies pushed by IT.
  • A file named managed-settings.json in a system folder: /Library/Application Support/ClaudeCode/ on macOS, /etc/claude-code/ on Linux and WSL, C:\Program Files\ClaudeCode\ on Windows. An optional managed-settings.d/ folder beside it holds drop-in files.

A developer can check what is enforced by running /status in Claude Code: the Setting sources line names the managed source in use.

Useful keys for an organisation

KeyWhat it does
permissions.denyBlock actions everywhere, for example reading .env files. A deny at any level cannot be overridden.
permissions.disableBypassPermissionsModeSet to "disable" so nobody can switch off permission prompts entirely.

A few keys work the other way round

For some security-sensitive keys, Claude Code honours a stricter value from a lower level even if managed settings say otherwise. For example, a developer who sets disableClaudeAiConnectors to true keeps connectors off even if the organisation enabled them. The rule: anyone can make things safer, nobody below the organisation can make them less safe.

Lab: draft and test a QA team's Claude Code policy30 min · your computer with Claude Code (any plan that includes it)

You will write a draft policy file and test it for one session using --settings, which applies settings above your own files without installing anything system-wide. This does not need admin rights. (A real deployment would go into managed settings, which only administrators can install.)

  1. Make a practice folder and a fake secret file:
    Terminal
    mkdir policy-lab && cd policy-lab
echo "API_TOKEN=not-a-real-token" > .env
echo "# Policy lab" > README.md
    A folder containing .env and README.md.
  2. Create org-policy-draft.json in the same folder with this content:
    org-policy-draft.json
    {
  "$schema": "https://json.schemastore.org/claude-code-settings.json",
  "availableModels": ["opus", "sonnet", "haiku"],
  "companyAnnouncements": ["QA policy: test accounts only. Never paste production customer data."],
  "cleanupPeriodDays": 14,
  "permissions": {
    "deny": ["Read(./.env)", "Read(./.env.*)", "Read(./secrets/**)"],
    "disableBypassPermissionsMode": "disable"
  }
}
    A strict JSON file: no comments, no trailing commas.
  3. Check the JSON is valid: python3 -m json.tool org-policy-draft.json.The file printed back neatly. An error such as Expecting ',' delimiter means a typo; fix it and run again.
  4. Start Claude Code with the draft applied: claude --settings ./org-policy-draft.json.The announcement text appears at startup.
  5. Type /status.The Setting sources line includes the command-line settings. If a managed source is also listed, your organisation already enforces a policy; note it.
  6. Type /model and look at the choices.Only models matching opus, sonnet and haiku are offered; Fable is not selectable. Press Esc to leave without changing.
  7. Ask: Read the .env file and tell me what is in it.Claude reports that reading .env is blocked by a permission rule, and does not show the token.
  8. Ask: Read README.md.It reads the file normally: the policy blocks secrets, not ordinary work.
  9. Exit with /exit. Write three lines for your admin: what you would add (for example a claudeMd with team conventions, or forceLoginOrgUUID), what you would remove, and why.A reviewed draft policy ready for an administrator to deploy as managed settings.

Troubleshooting: If Claude Code shows a Settings Error, the JSON is invalid; rerun step 3. If /model still offers everything, an embedding host or a managed source may supply its own model list; check /status. If claude isn't found, see Chapter 64. No Claude Code? Do steps 2 and 3 only, then annotate each line of the file with the risk it reduces.

Common misunderstandings
"Team and Enterprise get better models."
The same models are available. Organisation plans add administration, data terms and usage, not a smarter Claude.
"Enterprise is $20 a seat, so it's cheaper than Team."
Enterprise also bills usage at API rates on top of seats, has a 20-seat minimum and is billed annually. Compare total cost for your usage.
"Commercial plans never keep anything."
Default deletion is within 30 days for the API, chats are kept until deleted, and flagged content and feedback have longer retention. ZDR is a separate agreement.
"A developer can override the company's Claude Code settings with --settings."
Managed settings sit above everything, including --settings. Only stricter values for a few security keys can come from lower levels.
"If I can't see a feature, my account is broken."
Features vary by plan, region and admin choices. Ask your Owner.

42.7Review

Key takeaways
  • As of 9 November 2026: Free, Pro ($20), Max ($100), Ultra ($200) for individuals; Team (Standard $25 / Premium $125 per seat monthly, 2–150 people) and Enterprise ($20/seat annually, 20-seat minimum, plus usage at API rates) for organisations. Check current pricing.
  • Team adds SSO, domain verification, central billing, connector controls, usage analytics and organisation-wide skills. Enterprise adds SCIM, audit logs, role-based access, Compliance API, custom retention, domain capture, IP allowlisting and spend limits.
  • Commercial products are not used for training by default; API data is deleted within 30 days by default; ZDR and custom retention are agreed separately.
  • Managed settings for Claude Code override every personal and project setting and can arrive from the admin console, MDM or a system file.
  • Features differ by plan, region and admin settings: approve one route, write a short data rule and review it regularly.
Check yourself

Your company wants Claude accounts removed automatically when someone leaves, via the HR directory. Which feature, on which plan?

SCIM provisions and de-provisions accounts from the directory. The pricing page lists it for Enterprise, not Team.

By default, are conversations on a Team plan used to train Anthropic's models?

Commercial products are not used for training by default. Thumbs up/down feedback is the exception, and Owners can turn it off.

A developer runs claude --settings '{"permissions":{"allow":["Read(./.env)"]}}', but the organisation's managed settings deny reading .env. What happens?

Deny rules beat allow rules at any level, and managed settings have the highest precedence.

Where does a QA team best separate the API cost of its nightly CI triage job from other API use?

Console workspaces group API keys, members and limits, so costs can be tracked and capped per system.

A colleague on the same Team plan sees a feature you don't. What's the most likely cause?

Features vary by plan, region and administrator choices, and betas often roll out gradually.

Assignment

Write a one-page "Claude at our company" proposal for your QA team. Include: the plan you recommend and why (with today's prices and the date you checked them), the identity setup, the connectors you would allow, a three-line data rule for testers, a draft Claude Code managed-settings file based on the lab, and the questions you need your security team to answer (for example, whether ZDR or custom retention is needed). Ask one person from security and one from finance to review it.

Teach it

"Personal Claude plans are like personal phones. Team and Enterprise are the company fleet: same models, but we log in with company SSO, admins decide which connectors are allowed, we see usage, and our conversations aren't used for training by default.
