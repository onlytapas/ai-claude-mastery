OpenAI is the company that brought large language models to the public with ChatGPT in November 2022. It started in 2015 as a non-profit research lab, added a profit-making arm to raise money, and since October 2025 has been a public benefit corporation controlled by a non-profit foundation, with Microsoft owning about 27%. Its GPT models helped prove that scaling up next-word prediction produces broadly capable AI, and its products now range from ChatGPT to the Codex coding agent and a developer API. It is one of Anthropic's main competitors, and many of Anthropic's founders once worked there. Understanding OpenAI helps you understand the whole industry, and the tools your colleagues may already be using.

27.1Founding and early years (2015–2019)

OpenAI was founded in December 2015 in San Francisco as a non-profit research organisation. Its founders included Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Ilya Sutskever, Greg Brockman and Trevor Blackwell, among others. Its stated aim was to make sure advanced AI benefits all of humanity, and in its early years it published research openly.

  1. Dec 2015OpenAI founded as a non-profit.
  2. 2018GPT-1: "Improving Language Understanding by Generative Pre-Training" shows that pre-training a Transformer on lots of text, then fine-tuning, works well across tasks (Chapter 6).
  3. 2019GPT-2: much larger. OpenAI initially held back the full model over misuse concerns, then released it in stages, an early public argument about AI release policy.
  4. 2019The "capped-profit" arm. Training large models needed far more money than donations could provide. OpenAI created a for-profit subsidiary whose investors' returns were capped, still controlled by the non-profit. Microsoft made its first investment the same year.
In everyday life

Picture a charity that runs a research hospital, then realises it needs billions for new equipment. It sets up a company to sell services and attract investors, while the charity's board keeps control. That arrangement can work, but it creates a lasting tension: are decisions made for the mission or for the investors? Much of OpenAI's later history is about that tension.

27.2Scaling up and the ChatGPT moment (2020–2023)

  • 2020: GPT-3. The paper "Language Models are Few-Shot Learners" (Brown et al.) showed that a big enough model could do new tasks from just a few examples in the prompt. OpenAI offered it through a paid API rather than releasing the weights, a business model it still uses.
  • 2021–2022: images and code. CLIP linked images and text; DALL-E generated images from descriptions. OpenAI's Codex model powered the first version of GitHub Copilot.
  • 2022: InstructGPT. OpenAI showed that RLHF (training with human feedback, Chapter 18) made a model much better at following instructions, even when smaller.
  • 30 November 2022: ChatGPT. A chat interface on an RLHF-trained model, released as a "research preview". It became one of the fastest-adopted consumer products ever, and started the race described in Chapter 7.
  • March 2023: GPT-4. A large step in capability, and the first GPT to accept images as input.
Connection to Anthropic

Anthropic was founded in 2021 by former OpenAI staff, including Dario Amodei and Daniela Amodei. The two companies share research roots and compete directly today. Chapter 37 tells Anthropic's side of the story.

27.3Structure, governance and the Microsoft relationship

OpenAI's structure is unusual, and it has changed more than once.

  1. Nov 2023Board crisis. The non-profit board removed Sam Altman as CEO, saying he had not been consistently candid with it. Within days, after most staff threatened to leave and Microsoft offered to hire them, Altman returned and the board was reshaped. It showed how hard it is for a mission-focused board to control a company worth many billions.
  2. Oct 2025Recapitalisation. The for-profit arm became OpenAI Group PBC, a public benefit corporation, controlled by the non-profit OpenAI Foundation. Microsoft received a stake of about 27%.
  3. Apr 2026Microsoft deal rewritten (Apr 27). Microsoft's licence to OpenAI's technology is now non-exclusive, through 2032. OpenAI may serve products on any cloud, with Azure staying primary and getting launches first. The "AGI clause" was removed. OpenAI keeps paying a 20% revenue share, now capped, through 2030.

A public benefit corporation is a for-profit company that is legally required to weigh a stated public benefit alongside shareholders' interests. Anthropic is also a public benefit corporation, though its governance differs (Chapter 37).

Under the hood: what was the "AGI clause"?

Under earlier versions of the Microsoft deal, Microsoft's rights to OpenAI's technology were tied to whether OpenAI had achieved AGI (artificial general intelligence). That created an odd incentive: a technical milestone with no agreed definition could change billions of dollars of commercial rights. The April 2026 amendment removed the clause, replacing it with fixed dates (licence through 2032, revenue share capped through 2030). It's a useful reminder that "AGI" is used in contracts and marketing as well as science, and that its meaning depends on who's using it.

27.4The GPT line today

After GPT-4 came GPT-4o (2024), the "o-series" reasoning models starting with o1 in 2024 (Chapter 19), GPT-5 (2025) and GPT-5.5 (April 2026). OpenAI's naming has often been confusing; in 2026 it began adding a name to each model.

Notice that GPT-6.1 Sol and Terra are newer than GPT-6 Astra but are not more capable: they're cheaper and faster. A higher version number does not always mean a stronger model. A planned GPT-6.1 Astra was reportedly cancelled over safety regressions; that comes from press reports, not an OpenAI statement.

Check before you quote

Model names, prices and "most capable" labels change often. The table above was checked against OpenAI's models documentation on 9 November 2026. Some model names you may see online (for example "GPT-6 Cyber") appear only on aggregator sites and are not confirmed. Use OpenAI's own models page as the source of truth, and compare models on your own tasks (Chapter 36).

The business model is a mix: ChatGPT subscriptions for individuals and companies, pay-per-token API usage, and large enterprise deals. Its computing comes mainly from Microsoft Azure, plus a growing list of other suppliers: the Stargate project (with Oracle, SoftBank and MGX, announced January 2025), a large Oracle compute deal (September 2025), AMD chips (October 2025), AWS (November 2025), a custom chip with Broadcom, and NVIDIA (Chapter 25, Chapter 26).

Real-world example

In a typical QA department in 2026, a tester might use ChatGPT to draft a test plan, a developer might use Codex in the terminal, and the automation team might use Claude Code with Playwright MCP. That's normal. What matters is that the team has shared rules about data, review and verification that apply whichever tool is used (Chapter 119), and a way of comparing tools on its own work (Chapter 36).

27.6Research contributions

Whatever you think of its commercial path, OpenAI's research shaped the field:

  • The GPT series popularised large generative pre-training (GPT-1, GPT-2) and showed few-shot learning at scale (GPT-3).
  • Scaling laws: "Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models" (Kaplan et al., 2020) described how performance improves predictably with more data, parameters and compute (Chapter 138).
  • RLHF at scale through InstructGPT and ChatGPT (Chapter 18).
  • CLIP and DALL-E for linking and generating images and text (Chapter 21).
  • Reasoning models: the o1 line (2024) made "thinking before answering" a mainstream product feature (Chapter 19).
  • Benchmarks: OpenAI created SWE-bench Verified (August 2024), a human-checked set of real coding tasks. In February 2026 it stopped reporting it, citing contamination and flawed tasks, and recommended SWE-bench Pro instead (Chapter 24).

27.7Strengths and limits: a balanced view

Widely cited strengths

  • The best-known consumer AI brand; ChatGPT is many people's first AI tool
  • A broad product range: chat, coding, images, research, browser, API
  • A long record of influential research
  • Large computing commitments from many suppliers

Widely cited concerns

  • A governance history with sudden changes (the 2023 board crisis, repeated restructuring)
  • Confusing model naming and frequent product changes
  • Less openness than its name suggests: frontier models are closed, though gpt-oss is open-weight
  • Large financial commitments that depend on continued fast growth
Opinion · views on OpenAI differ

Supporters, including OpenAI's leadership, argue that shipping products widely and early is the best way for society to adapt to AI. Critics, including some former employees and AI-safety researchers, argue that commercial pressure has pushed safety down the priority list. Some of Anthropic's founders left OpenAI partly over differences in approach. These are views held by those people and groups; this textbook doesn't rank the companies, and you should judge tools on evidence from your own use.

Common misunderstandings
"OpenAI is a non-profit."
It started as one. Today the operating company is OpenAI Group PBC, a for-profit public benefit corporation controlled by the non-profit OpenAI Foundation.
"Microsoft owns OpenAI."
Microsoft holds about 27%. Since April 2026 its licence is non-exclusive and OpenAI can use other clouds.
"OpenAI invented the Transformer."
The Transformer came from Google researchers in 2017. OpenAI's GPT models applied it to generative pre-training at scale.
"The newest GPT number is always the strongest."
GPT-6.1 Sol and Terra are newer than GPT-6 Astra but positioned as cheaper and faster, not more capable.
"ChatGPT and Claude can't share project instructions."
Codex reads AGENTS.md, and Claude Code can read or import it, so one file can serve both.

27.8Review

Key takeaways
  • OpenAI was founded in December 2015 as a non-profit, added a capped-profit arm in 2019, and became OpenAI Group PBC, controlled by the OpenAI Foundation, in October 2025.
  • Microsoft holds about 27%; the April 2026 amendment made its licence non-exclusive and removed the AGI clause.
  • ChatGPT (November 2022) launched the generative AI boom; GPT-4 (2023), the o-series (2024) and GPT-5 (2025) followed.
  • As of 9 November 2026: GPT-6 Astra is the flagship; GPT-6.1 Sol and Terra are newer, cheaper and faster.
  • Main products are ChatGPT, Codex and the API; revenue comes from subscriptions, API usage and enterprise deals.
  • Key research: GPT series, scaling laws, RLHF at scale, CLIP and DALL-E, reasoning models.
  • AGENTS.md lets Codex and Claude Code share one set of project rules.
