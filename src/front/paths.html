Where to start

You don't need to read every chapter with the same care. The book is organised in five stages, from understanding AI to engineering it. Honest timing: the whole book is about {{H_ALL}} hours of reading and practice ({{H_CORE}} hours without the optional chapters), before assignments and projects. Read it in order, or jump straight to the part you need; the goals below suggest where to start.

Reading the labels

Every chapter shows three labels at the top:

  • Level (Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Expert, Reference): how much background it assumes.
  • Goal: Goal · Understand means you'll be able to explain a concept; Goal · Use means you'll be able to operate a tool; Goal · Build means you'll be able to create a working system yourself. These are different skills, and interviews test all three.
  • Optional marks reference material (every flag, every shortcut, long catalogues) and expert-only topics (training large models, deep infrastructure). Look these up when you need them; they're not on the main path.
Don't memorise everything

Learn the major milestones of AI history, not every date. Learn the commands you use often through practice; search the reference chapters for the rest. Spend your effort on understanding, practice and projects.
